Rural Recycling Access (RIG6)

Automated CRV redemption infrastructure for tribal, rural, and underserved communities.

Project Overview

The Rural Recycling Access project is a 24‑month, 18‑site automated beverage container redemption network funded through CalRecycle’s Beverage Container Redemption Innovation Grant Program — Round 6 (RIG6). CII deploys Reverse Vending Machine (RVM) kiosks across tribal lands, high schools, and healthcare institutions to close the infrastructure gap that has historically excluded rural and tribal residents from California’s CRV system.

Where We Operate

The network includes fully autonomous kiosks at tribal centers, high schools, and hospitals, along with semi‑automated kiosks at smaller tribal communities and regional healthcare sites. Together, these locations create a county‑wide redemption backbone for Humboldt and adjacent service areas.

Key Components

  • 18 RVM Kiosks: 12 fully autonomous and 6 semi‑automated kiosks serving tribal nations, schools, and hospitals.
  • Zero‑Emission Fleet: Six Rivian commercial EV cargo trucks providing collection and logistics.
  • Off‑Grid Solar & Battery: Solar arrays and battery banks at priority tribal sites to ensure reliable, generator‑free operation.
  • School & Hospital Integration: On‑site CRV access paired with education and sustainability commitments.

Community Benefit

This project is designed as an environmental justice infrastructure investment. It increases CRV redemption access, reduces landfill disposal, and returns CRV value to households, youth, and tribal communities who have long paid into the system without practical access to redemption.
